Knowledge Center

BioBlock Glossary

Plain-language definitions for the technologies, standards, and governance concepts used across BioBlock.

Knowledge CenterFAQ
AI — Artificial Intelligence
Machine-based simulation of human intelligence for pattern recognition and decision-making.
API — Application Programming Interface
Rules and tools that allow different software applications to communicate and interact.
Audit Trail
A chronological record of data changes that supports integrity, compliance, and traceability.
BioX — Biotech Tokenization
Blockchain-based representation of biological assets or processes.
Blockchain
A distributed digital ledger that records transactions securely, transparently, and immutably across network nodes.
CA — Certificate Authority
An authority that issues digital certificates to authenticate participants.
Fabric CA
Hyperledger Fabric’s certificate authority service. For BioBlock, it can provide cryptographic proof of membership and access rights.
Chaincode
Smart contracts in Hyperledger Fabric, commonly written in Go, Java, or Node.js. They can define logic for data trading, IP-NFT minting, and fee distribution.
Consortium Blockchain
A permissioned blockchain validated by a defined group of members, such as industry, SMEs, CDMOs, research institutes, and public actors.
CP — Community Project
A project led and managed by a community of stakeholders rather than one centralized organization.
CPV — Continued Process Verification
Ongoing monitoring of manufacturing performance to ensure consistency.
CSV — Computer System Validation
Evidence that digital systems used in bioprocessing meet regulatory standards.
CtD — Compute-to-Data
A model in which algorithms run where the data is stored, without moving the data itself. This can support sensitive or large biotech datasets.
DAO — Decentralized Autonomous Organization
A governance system that uses on-chain smart contracts and community voting.
dApp — Decentralized Application
An application that runs on a blockchain or decentralized network without a single point of control.
Data Marketplace
A platform for trading data assets. In BioBlock, this is a core function for research data represented as IP-NFTs and Datatokens.
dCoP — Decentralized Community of Practice
A community in which professionals develop knowledge and standards through decentralized participation.
DID — Decentralized Identifier
A self-sovereign identity standard for secure digital authentication.
DIN SPEC
A German fast-track standardization instrument that can define metadata and interface standards for biotech data trading.
DLT — Distributed Ledger Technology
An umbrella term for technologies, including blockchain, that distribute records across multiple nodes.
DMP — Data Management Plan
A plan for how data is collected, stored, shared, and preserved.
Ethereum
A blockchain platform with smart contract functionality and the source of standards such as ERC-20 and ERC-721.
ERC-20
A common Ethereum standard for fungible tokens.
ERC-721
An Ethereum standard for unique, non-fungible digital assets.
FAIR / FAIR Data
Principles that make research data Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable.
FBC — Future Bioprocessing Center
A center focused on advancing bioprocess technologies.
GDPR
The European Union’s General Data Protection Regulation. BioBlock can keep sensitive data off-chain while storing hashes and metadata on-chain.
GMP — Good Manufacturing Practice
Requirements for compliant scaling and manufacture of biomedical innovations.
High Throughput
Automated processing of large volumes of data or samples.
Hyperledger
An open-source Linux Foundation project providing modular frameworks for permissioned blockchains.
Hyperledger Fabric
An enterprise-grade permissioned blockchain framework suitable for modular consortium governance and access control.
Identity Management
Systems for managing digital identities in decentralized environments.
Inclusion
Promoting diversity and equal access in innovation and governance.
Interoperability
The ability of systems to exchange and understand data. BioBlock initially focuses on semantic interoperability through metadata standards.
IPFS — InterPlanetary File System
A decentralized protocol for storing and sharing files across a distributed network.
IP-NFT — Intellectual Property Non-Fungible Token
A tokenized representation of IP rights used to express ownership, licensing, and access.
Layer 1
A foundational blockchain protocol such as Ethereum or Bitcoin.
Layer 2
A framework built on a Layer 1 network to improve speed or reduce transaction costs.
Living Lab
A real-world innovation environment used to test technologies in practice.
MSP — Membership Service Provider
A Hyperledger component that manages participant identities and roles, ensuring only authorized members can validate or trade.
Metadata
Descriptive information that improves data discovery, organization, and context.
Minting
The process of creating a token or NFT. In BioBlock, data owners or an operator may mint IP-NFTs and Datatokens.
ML — Machine Learning
An AI technique for learning patterns from data and making predictions.
MT — Management Team
The core coordination body overseeing the roadmap, compliance, and community project implementation.
MVP — Minimum Viable Product
A version of a platform or workflow sufficient to test key assumptions with early adopters.
NFT — Non-Fungible Token
A unique digital asset recorded on a blockchain.
P2P — Peer-to-Peer
Direct interaction between users without a centralized server.
PAT — Process Analytical Technology
Real-time monitoring tools for understanding and controlling bioprocesses.
Peers
Nodes that maintain a ledger and execute chaincode. Universities, SMEs, or CDMOs can run peer nodes.
Permission Management
A system for managing user rights and data access.
PKI — Public Key Infrastructure
A cryptographic system for secure digital communication.
PnP — Plug and Play
A modular system design that supports flexible integration in biomanufacturing.
PoA — Proof of Authority
A consensus mechanism in which authorized validators create blocks; efficient for consortium blockchains.
PoS — Proof of Stake
A consensus mechanism that selects validators based on token holdings or stake.
PoW — Proof of Work
A consensus mechanism that requires computational effort to validate transactions.
Prototyping
Creating preliminary models of systems or products for testing and learning.
QC — Quality Control
Procedures that ensure a product or dataset meets defined standards.
Regulatory Foresight
Anticipating future regulation to guide responsible innovation.
SAB — Stakeholder Advisory Board
A group of stakeholders that provides strategic advice, oversight, and guidance.
SDK — Software Development Kit
Tools and libraries used to build applications, including decentralized applications.
Smart Contract
A self-executing agreement whose rules are written in code. It can govern trading, fee distribution, governance, and upgrades.
Social Innovation
A novel approach to addressing social challenges, with or without technology.
SSI — Self-Sovereign Identity
An identity model in which individuals control their own digital credentials.
Sustainability
Designing systems for environmental and social resource efficiency.
Tokenization
Converting a physical or digital asset into a blockchain-based token.
TPS — Transactions Per Second
A metric used to measure blockchain throughput and scalability.
TRL — Technology Readiness Level
A maturity scale for technology; TRL 6 indicates demonstration of a prototype in a relevant environment.
Upgradability
The ability to update smart contracts through controlled proxy mechanisms and governance.
Validation
Confirming that a system or process performs reliably and consistently.
Valorisation
Increasing the value of an asset, idea, or resource through development, commercialization, or recognition.
VC — Verifiable Credential
A digitally signed credential used in decentralized identity systems.
Web3
A model of the internet built on decentralized technologies, emphasizing ownership, privacy, and blockchain-based interaction.
Web3 Governance
Decentralized mechanisms for managing rules, identity, and decisions.
Web3 Gateway
An interface connecting users and apps to blockchain networks, smart contracts, and decentralized storage such as IPFS.
ZKP — Zero-Knowledge Proof
A cryptographic method for verifying a claim without revealing the underlying data.
