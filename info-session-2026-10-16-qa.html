Information Session Q&A

Questions and answers from the second BioBlock Call for Proposals information session, including follow-up questions.

1. Involvement of Industry Partners & Network Contacts

Question: Is an industry partner mandatory when applying for a Community Sprint, and is there a matchmaking platform or directory to find partners?

Answer:

  • An industry partner is not mandatory for academic applicants. A single academic institution may apply if it can demonstrate the expertise needed to address the selected challenge.
  • Whether an industry partner strengthens an application depends on the project idea and how the applicant demonstrates its ability to deliver the proposed work. No general assessment bonus for including an industry partner was specified.
  • A matchmaking system had been planned but had not been established.

For the partner requirements of each project format, please refer to Annex 2: Project Formats.

Follow-up question: Is there at least a contact directory or another way for newcomers to reach potential partners?

Answer:

  • The BioBlock team offered to circulate an expression of interest to the entire Stakeholder Advisory Board or wider community.
  • The team could not offer individual matchmaking with a selected partner; the information would need to be shared with everyone.
  • No partner directory was provided during the exchange. This does not establish that no directory exists.
2. Necessity of Blockchain Expertise & Technology

Question: Do all subprojects need to be built directly on blockchain technology?

Answer:

  • No, not every subproject needs to implement blockchain technology itself. The FAIR AI tool, for example, can support the BioBlock platform without being built directly on blockchain.
  • Core blockchain prototype development is primarily covered by CP3. Other functions, such as negotiation through smart contracts, have blockchain-related requirements.
  • All projects should contribute to the overall BioBlock platform. Participants are also expected to show willingness to participate in the decentralized Community of Practice.

Follow-up question: For the metadata challenge, Challenge 4, is the objective to generate metadata using blockchain, or to make metadata usable in a blockchain environment?

Answer:

  • The participant suggested that metadata could be used within a blockchain environment without blockchain itself generating the metadata.
  • The presenter explicitly agreed with this distinction.

Question: Does the Principal Investigator (PI) need extensive prior experience in blockchain technology, or can broader knowledge applicable to the proposed work be sufficient?

Answer:

  • The Great Council will assess expertise against the specific tasks and deliverables of the proposal.
  • Applicants must demonstrate that the work will be carried out by people with the relevant expertise. Developing blockchain technology requires appropriate blockchain expertise; other tasks require the expertise relevant to those tasks.
  • No universal minimum number of years of blockchain experience or separate PI-specific threshold was stated.
3. Community Onboarding & User Acquisition

Question: How do new members or users join the decentralized Community of Practice (particularly in the context of Community Project 2)?

Answer:

  • The respondent linked the question to the earlier discussion about contacting users and explained how the community is developing.
  • CP1 established conceptual foundations, CP3–CP5 develop the technology layer, and CP2 develops the governance layer and connects the trust layers.
  • The community is expected to grow through participating stakeholders and new Community Projects funded through the challenges.
  • BioBlock is progressively moving from the more centralized arrangements needed to establish the initiative towards a decentralized Community of Practice.

Interested in joining the community? You can contact the BioBlock Management Team at any time at info@bioblock-community.org to express your interest in participating.

4. Funding Eligibility of Travel Costs for External Guests

Question: Can a project reimburse travel expenses for external community members invited to a workshop at the applicant’s institution to provide feedback?

Answer:

  • Travel costs were described as eligible budget items in principle.
  • However, the specific arrangement of paying external participants’ travel expenses requires clarification with and approval by PtJ.

Follow-up question: Does this also cover participants travelling to our workshop, rather than our own project team travelling elsewhere?

Answer:

  • The applicant proposed including the costs in the application subject to further approval if the proposal progressed. The presenter acknowledged this conditional understanding.
  • This was not an approval of the costs themselves.
  • Another participant reported that similar reimbursements had previously been possible but had become more difficult. They recommended asking PtJ specifically about its current approach and providing a clear justification.
  • The proposed reimbursement arrangement should be explicitly clarified with PtJ before treating the costs as accepted.

For the funding conditions of each project format, please refer to Annex 2: Project Formats.

5. Organizational Framework & Budget Rules

The presenters provided the following additional guidance during the discussion:

Alignment with the Challenge:

  • Applicants should carefully explain how their proposal addresses the selected challenge and its specifications.
  • An otherwise strong proposal may still be unsuitable if it is not sufficiently aligned with the challenge.

Number of Funded Projects:

  • The organisers expect to fund one project per challenge, explaining that they do not intend to establish competing Community Projects addressing the same challenge.
  • This expresses the intended selection approach and should not be read as a guarantee that a project will be funded under every challenge.

Budgets & Overheads:

  • The stated partner limits include up to €150,000 per partner for Community Sprints and up to €250,000 for an academic partner in a Practice Sprint.
  • The higher academic limit does not apply to non-academic partners.
  • The maximum total budget for the selected challenge must always be respected, alongside the applicable partner limit.
  • All maximum budgets include overheads; overheads cannot be added on top of these caps.
  • Applicants with questions about budget preparation were invited to contact the BioBlock team.

For details on project formats, partner limits, funding rates and overheads, please refer to Annex 2: Project Formats.

Legal Seat:

  • The presentation stated that direct funding recipients must have a legal seat in Germany.
  • Organisations without a legal seat in Germany were described as potentially participating as unfunded Associated Partners or as Subcontractors. The presenter also stated that Subcontractors would not receive rights to use the project results.
  • This information came from the presentation rather than a separate audience question.
6. Availability of Presentation Slides

Question: Will the presentation slides be shared with attendees?

Answer:

  • Yes, the presenter committed to emailing the slides to all registered participants.
  • Publication on the website was mentioned as a possibility, rather than a confirmed publication commitment.
  • No specific circulation or publication date was given.

Additional information:

  • The organisers also said they would try to publish answers to the session’s questions on the website and invited participants to contact them with further questions.

Further questions? Contact info@bioblock-community.org.
