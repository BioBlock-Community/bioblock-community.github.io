Knowledge Center

Frequently Asked Questions

BioBlock governance, advisory roles, community projects, and funding eligibility.

Knowledge CenterGlossary
What are the tasks and responsibilities of the SAB?

The Stakeholder Advisory Board provides strategic advice, reviews major proposals, advocates externally, validates roadmaps, and helps shape policy in areas such as ethics, regulation, and sustainability.

To what extent should the SAB be involved in daily work and planning?

The SAB offers high-level strategic advice rather than daily operational management. Members can, however, take active roles in shaping community projects or policy discussions in areas of enhanced engagement.

What is the Great Council?

The Great Council is BioBlock’s community decision body and consists of active community members. It votes on funding, major platform changes, and policy. Its hybrid governance model combines structured dialogue, documented decisions, and blockchain-based tools where useful.

What form of governance does BioBlock use?

A hybrid Web3 model: the Management Team handles operations, the Stakeholder Advisory Board provides strategic guidance, and the Great Council makes democratic decisions. A governance charter defines processes, quorums, and escalation, while smart contracts automate actions where appropriate.

Can reviewing processes or event participation be compensated?

These activities are currently voluntary. BioBlock is exploring ways to recognize expert contributions in the future, ranging from honoraria to token-based recognition.

What are “areas of enhanced engagement and increased visibility”?

SAB members may become more closely involved in thematic projects, workshops, or working groups aligned with their expertise. Increased visibility can include presenting at events, appearing in consortium communications, or shaping thought-leadership work.

What does participation in community projects involve?

Participation is flexible: reviewing proposals, mentoring teams, helping projects navigate challenges, or actively contributing to a community project. Funder rules may limit partner eligibility or require minimum budget and contribution thresholds.

Can non-German organizations be funded?

No. Only institutions based in Germany are eligible for direct funding. Non-German organizations can participate financially only as subcontractors.

Can proposals be submitted in English?

BioBlock accepts English proposals for the first decision process to support inclusion. Specific funder rules from PtJ or national agencies may later require German-language documents and local partner involvement.
